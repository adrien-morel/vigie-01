# Product scoping — VIGIE-01

A scoping document written before development. Aim: to set out the problem, the perimeter and the success criteria before writing any code.

## 1. Context

In the defence industry, tracking export-control risk and geopolitical news is done, in most organisations, by hand: a daily press review, scattered email alerts, no structured record of source reliability. Two typical consequences: a long human reaction time in the face of dense, multilingual news, and no traceability of how information surfaced (it is impossible to justify after the fact why a piece of information was kept or discarded).

**Target users**: analysts in export compliance, competitive intelligence and public affairs functions.

## 2. Problem statement

> How do you cut the daily synthesis time for export and geopolitical risk, while standardising how weak signals are read and tracing the reliability of every piece of information surfaced — without degrading the quality of the final human judgement?

The critical point is not collection (already partly automatable with existing watch tools) but:
1. consistent **classification** of a heterogeneous stream of sources,
2. **multi-source cross-checking** to tell a confirmed signal from an isolated rumour,
3. systematic **traceability** (every statement must be backed by one or more identified sources).

## 3. Expected value

| Dimension | Value | Measurable through |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Reduction of the daily synthesis time (manual review → reading a structured digest) | End-to-end processing time per cycle |
| Quality | Standardised reading of weak signals (a consistent classification grid, independent of whichever analyst is on duty) | Classification precision on an annotated sample |
| Reliability | Systematic source → statement traceability, an explicit confidence score | Rate of cross-checked events vs single-source ones |
| Coverage | Widening the perimeter watched without a proportional human effort | Source coverage (active / targeted) |

**An explicit non-goal**: the system makes no decision (no automatic blocking of a contract, no enforceable regulatory alert). It increases the speed and rigour of the human watch — the decision stays human. That is an ethical and legal point of vigilance to be assumed explicitly in the product's positioning.

### An illustrative order of magnitude (sizing assumptions, to be validated with the using organisation)

| Item | Assumption | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Team concerned | Analysts dedicated to the export/geopolitics watch | 4 |
| Current manual review time | A scattered press review, with no aggregation | ~90 min/day/analyst |
| Review time with a structured digest | Targeted reading, prioritised by category and source | ~30 min/day/analyst |
| Time saved | 60 min/day/analyst × 4 analysts × ~220 working days/year | ~528 h/year |
| Fully loaded analyst cost (market order of magnitude) | Assumption | ~€60/h |
| **Estimated value of that time** | 528 h × €60 | **~€32,000/year** |
| **Real cost of the system (measured)** | ~200 items/day × 1 classification call, plus the verifier's escalation (capped); cost per call measured in real conditions | **~€140/year** (upper bound ~€160/year if escalation saturates its caps) |

The cost per call ($0.0018, measured: ~667 input tokens / ~231 output) is stable; it is volume that drives the bill. Reworking the source selection (§4) roughly doubled the daily volume without changing the order of magnitude of the cost — at this scale, the real spending line is not mass classification but the verifier's agentic escalation, whose cost is proportional to the number of items escalated and not to the volume collected. That is the reason for the two caps documented in §8.

The value/cost ratio stays favourable even under degraded assumptions (for example, the time saved halved). In any case, most of the expected benefit is not time alone but the standardised reading of weak signals and systematic traceability — qualitative, harder to monetise, and just as decisive in an adoption decision.

## 4. MECE scoping of the perimeter

### Included (V1)
- **Themes**: export control (licences, sanctions, embargoes), arms contracts, military movements, defence diplomacy, industrial programmes.
- **Geographic area**: worldwide — the MECE filtering is thematic only (see the revision below), and each item's location is extracted as metadata without restricting collection.
- **Sources**: specialised defence press, official communiqués, public RSS feeds — selected by country, see the methodology below.
- **Languages**: English, French, German, Italian, Spanish (classification is multilingual; the display is in English since the pass of 2026-09-06, in French before it).
- **Time horizon**: a daily watch (a 96 h freshness window at collection — 48 h originally, widened on 2026-08-17 once the cost risk it covered was taken over by a per-source cap, see the methodology below), no deep history in V1.

#### Source selection methodology

The first list of sources (5 feeds) had been assembled by availability — which exposed an accepted imbalance towards American and French-language news. The selection was reworked on an explicit, citable criterion rather than on convenience:

1. **A geographic backbone**: the world's top 10 arms exporters according to the SIPRI ranking (*Trends in International Arms Transfers*, 2020-24 data), which account for ~89% of world exports. That ranking directly covers three of the perimeter's five categories (contracts, industrial programmes, movements).
2. **A separate addition for export control**: Iran and North Korea, absent from a ranking by export volume but central to the `export_control` category (regimes under active embargo). They are added as a distinct fixed list, not artificially folded into the top 10.
3. **A quota by *type* of source rather than by raw count**: aiming, per country, for one institutional source and one or two specialised press sources. A strictly equal number of feeds per country would equalise the sampling effort without equalising real representativeness — the volume of defence news produced by the United States and France is objectively greater than that of Brazil or South Korea.
4. **Empirical validation before integration**: every candidate feed is tested live (availability, format validity, freshness) before entering `backend/config.py`. Of 19 candidates identified, 7 turned out to be unusable (403/404 errors, DNS, invalid certificate) and were replaced with tested alternatives.

**Handling state sources.** For China, Russia, Iran and North Korea, the only free RSS feeds available are state media or semi-official agencies. Three options presented themselves: exclude them (a blind spot on countries central to export control), include them without distinction (an undeclared propaganda bias), or include them while explicitly marking their provenance. The third was chosen: a `state_affiliated` field is carried by the source and propagated through to the digest, in the same traceability logic as the verified quote (§8) — the information is still collected, but its nature is visible to the analyst rather than lost in the general stream.

#### Fix of 2026-08-17: a per-source cap, and the replacement of a dead feed

The quota by *type* of source (point 3 above) remained declarative: nothing bounded the raw volume of a high-cadence feed, while cost is paid per item collected. Measured in real conditions: TASS alone produced ~35% of the volume collected (48 h) and ~45% of the digest, at under 1.5 LLM calls per item actually kept; conversely the four American feeds — 43% of world exports, the largest share of the perimeter — fell outside the freshness window then in force (48 h) every other day, their publication cadence being slower than that of press agency feeds. The budget was allocated by what each feed publishes, not by its value to the watch.

Two fixes, measured before and after on the day's real feeds (with no LLM call — RSS reading only, `backend.agents.collector.collect()`/`source_freshness()` called directly): a per-source cap at collection time (`MAX_ITEMS_PER_SOURCE_PER_RUN`, `backend/config.py`), which keeps only the most recent items of a feed; and widening the freshness window to 96 h, made possible by that same cap — the cost risk a short window bounded indirectly (submitting a whole institutional feed's backlog) is now bounded directly, per feed, independently of time. Before (original config, 48 h window, no cap): 256 items, TASS alone 35.5% of the volume, 12/17 sources active. After (new config, 96 h window, per-source cap): 138 items, largest contributor at 8.7% (four feeds tied at the cap of 12), 17/18 sources active.

Three generalist feeds (a national press agency, no dedicated defence desk) receive a reduced cap rather than the default: CGTN, Jerusalem Post, Yonhap. Justified by measurement on the annotated sample of §7 (n=68): an LLM-calls-per-kept-item ratio of 6 to infinity on those three feeds (CGTN: 6/6 items out_of_scope), against 1 to 2 on most other sources. Not removed — the only free coverage available for China, and Israel/South Korea have no usable institutional feed (see §11) — but capped at the yield they demonstrate.

OFAC (US Treasury), the only American `export_control` source, turned out to have been dead for over a year at the time of the observation (last entry 2025-07-01) without any existing check detecting it — a feed that parses without error counted as an "active source" for the coverage KPI (§7) while producing not a single item. Replaced by the Bureau of Industry and Security (Federal Register), which administers the Export Administration Regulations — a more direct target for the perimeter than the Treasury. Naval News (specialised naval press) added to reinforce American volume. The coverage KPI is corrected accordingly (§7): "active" now means "produced a recent item", measured at every campaign launch (`scripts/daily_run.py`) without waiting for the annual review of the list, not "the feed parses without error".

**Fix of 2026-08-18: a fourth capped feed.** The fix above had only measured three generalist feeds; re-reading the same annotated sample (n=68) to prepare the accumulation campaign, Mehr News showed 5/6 items `out_of_scope` — identical to Jerusalem Post's ratio — without having received a reduced cap, and was discarding 18 items/96 h that day at the default cap. Corrected by the same rule (capped at 8, like Jerusalem Post) rather than reopened: the ratio measured is the one already used for the other three feeds, not a new threshold.

#### Revision: geographic filtering removed from the collection criterion

The initial version of the scoping: a perimeter restricted to the Atlantic-Mediterranean area, applied as a rejection criterion. Reviewed after testing on real data — at the time of that revision the source list held 5 feeds, before the by-country selection described above. Two of the five confirmed sources of the time (Breaking Defense, Defense News) publish mostly domestic American news without ever explicitly naming a country of the area in the text — strict geographic filtering at collection time would have rejected nearly all of their items, when that content (programmes, contracts, sanctions) is genuinely relevant to the export-control watch and the American Atlantic seaboard has no reason to be excluded from the area. Decision: the location (country/sea/region) is still extracted and verified per item — it is that field that carries the coverage map delivered in V2 (section 10) — but it no longer serves as a rejection criterion in V1. Geographic filtering remains possible downstream (display, V2) if the need is confirmed by use, rather than decided a priori at collection.

### Boundary clarifications (added after disagreements observed in annotation, see §7)

- **Mergers, acquisitions and equity stakes in the defence industry**: classified `industrial_program` if the article is about the transaction itself (parties, amount, strategic or sovereignty stake) — for example a buyout by a foreign entity liable to trigger an investment review. Classified `export_control` only if the article explicitly deals with a licence, sanction or embargo procedure. *Clarification of 2026-08-22*: the category is defined by the **legal instrument** and not by the economic effect. A customs duty, a tariff barrier or a general trade policy measure is not one, even when aimed at components with military uses: `out_of_scope`, unless they take the form of a licence, a sanction or an embargo. Without that bound, the category absorbs the news of trade tensions, the perimeter drift the exclusion list below exists precisely to block. Classified `out_of_scope` if the article centres on market analysis (share price, market reaction) rather than on the transaction.
- **Opinion pieces, op-eds or forward-looking analysis**: classified `out_of_scope`, even when the theme matches the perimeter, if it does not report a dated, verifiable fact or event — the watch is about sourced facts (see §2, "every statement must be backed by a source"), not about points of view. An article that reports a dated fact and adds analysis to it stays in; an article that is nothing but a statement of position does not.
- **`defense_diplomacy` vs `military_movement`** (added after a sample of 88 annotated items, the most frequent confusion in the sample — see §7): a statement, an official communiqué or a named official's remarks on posture, intentions or defence cooperation between states is classified `defense_diplomacy`, even when the subject raised is an armed force or a weapon — it is the nature of the act reported (a statement) that prevails over its subject. `military_movement` is reserved for the deployment, positioning or effective control **reported** of a force, a ship or a military asset, not for a comment on that deployment. *Clarification added after retesting the disputed items*: the rule "the statement prevails" clashed with "effective control **reported**", military control most often being established by an official statement. The split therefore bears on the content of what is declared and not on the form of the act: an accomplished operational fact or an established state of affairs (a force is deployed, a strait is closed or under control, a strike has taken place) belongs to `military_movement` including when it is reported through an official communiqué — the statement is then only the source that establishes the fact, not the subject of the article; an intention, a threat, a claimed capability, a posture or defence cooperation between states belong to `defense_diplomacy`. Declaring that a strait is controlled and threatening to close it are not the same act. *Measurement*: the first formulation of this rule, which stated the same distinction but placed the `defense_diplomacy` clause first, turned out to have no effect — a two-arm retest on identical input, 0 changes over 25 items. Only the rewording leading with the `military_movement` branch moved the reference case. The rule's position in the prompt therefore counted as much as its content. *Full retest (2026-08-17, 25 items, 50 calls)*: confirmed on the reference case (Strait of Hormuz) and on the 3 other originally disputed items — no regression introduced relative to the original formulation. Over the 21 controls, a raw gap (15/25 against 19/25 agreement with the original arm) first read as a broad regression, but a third arm isolating this rule alone (without the `industrial_program` rule, added separately after that retest, see below) showed that 5 of the 6 gaps are due to that one, not to this one. **One regression does remain attributable to this rule**: a bilateral military exercise already under way (Chile-Spain, the "Filo Andino" mountain exercise), correctly classified `military_movement` by the original rule, flips to `defense_diplomacy` — the mention of strengthening interoperability activates the defence-cooperation clause when the fact reported is an exercise in progress, not an announcement of cooperation. The rule does not distinguish a joint exercise already under way (an accomplished fact) from announced defence cooperation: both use the same vocabulary of cooperation. Status: validated on the reference case and nearly all the controls. *Gap fix (2026-08-17)*: a clause added stating that a joint military exercise or training already under way (troops deployed, manoeuvres in progress) belongs to `military_movement` even when described in the vocabulary of cooperation/interoperability — that vocabulary characterises the nature of the exercise under way, not a separate announcement of cooperation. It only switches to `defense_diplomacy` if no concrete exercise is yet under way (an agreement, partnership or intention to cooperate in future, with no manoeuvre in progress). *Lightweight retest* (the protocol of points 38/39, not the 50 calls of the full protocol): the regression case [68] (Filo Andino) returns to `military_movement`, and two controls with similar cooperation vocabulary but no exercise under way (NK News on US-ROK manoeuvres still to come, Mehr News on a joint defence agreement) correctly stay `defense_diplomacy` — no over-triggering observed on that restricted sample.
- **`defense_diplomacy` vs `out_of_scope`** (same sample): a statement or official communiqué attributed to a named official **who is in post** (or officially mandated), on cooperation, alliances or defence/security posture between states, is a dated fact — it is not classified `out_of_scope` on the sole ground that no contract or movement is described. Conversely, a state visit, a protocol message or general diplomatic pressure (human rights, the domestic politics of a third country) with no explicit defence/security content stays `out_of_scope`, even when the two countries otherwise have a defence relationship. *Clarification of 2026-08-22*: **official capacity at the time of the facts** is required. Remarks by a former official — a retired general officer, a former minister — commit no state: that is a statement of position, and therefore the case the opinion rule above excludes, not a statement of posture. What founds `defense_diplomacy` is that a state speaks through someone qualified to do so; failing that, the boundary with the expert op-ed disappears.
- **`arms_contract` vs `industrial_program`** *(written on 2026-08-22, after a second measurement pointed at this boundary — see §7; a rule had already been living in the classification prompt since 2026-08-17 without ever having been carried back here, see the drift note at the end of the section; **reworded on 2026-08-23**, see the rewording note at the end of this bullet)*: three stages, to be separated on **the subject of the article** and never on the visible actor, **and in this order**. **First test — the transaction and its money**: does the article report a commercial or budgetary act? award or notification of a contract, order placed, framework agreement concluded, a government's acquisition decision, or the money behind it — funds voted, requested from parliament or released, a supplementary appropriation, down payments made to secure delivery timelines. If so, it is `arms_contract`, **including when the article justifies that act by delivery timelines, a programme schedule or a capability to be built**: the stated motive does not move the category, the act reported is what fixes it. **Second test, only if the first fails — manufacture and the life of the capability**: development, construction, launch or roll-out, delivery, entry into service, qualification trials, modernisation and through-life support belong to `industrial_program`, including when the military customer is named and the article uses the vocabulary of ordering. What precedes the transaction — request for information, consultation, preliminary study, force structure target — is `industrial_program`. A manufacturer delivering a first example to an armed service reports a programme milestone; the same manufacturer winning the contract reports a transaction. *An accepted rule change, dated 2026-08-22*: the previous formulation placed "a delivery" on the `arms_contract` side, which the model was applying correctly and which the annotation contradicts — the revision follows the parent definition of `industrial_program` (the stage of building a capability), of which a delivery is the culmination and not the transaction, signed elsewhere and earlier. *Rewording of 2026-08-23, on two distinct defects found by re-reading the reference case against the text of the rule*: **(a) position** — the `industrial_program` branch was stated first and expansively, so that an article naming delivery timelines activated it before the funding clause was reached; the split is now ordered and leads with `arms_contract`, the branch that should win that case, a direct application of the lesson of the 2026-08-17 retest. **(b) coverage** — the clause said "funds voted" when the reference case is about funds *requested* from parliament and not yet voted; it therefore did not literally cover the case it named. The two defects are independent and were fixed together.
- **Aerospace and civil technologies with an industrial component** *(added on 2026-08-22, after two items in the sample were annotated in opposite directions — see §7)*: an aerospace, space or civil technology article only enters the perimeter if it is explicitly tied to a defence application, a defence customer, or industrial cooperation involving a defence group. Avionics developed jointly with a defence group's subsidiary belongs to `industrial_program`; a commercial satellite launch by a private company, with no stated defence link, stays `out_of_scope`. The criterion is the defence link written in the article, not the presumed dual-use nature of the technology — without which the perimeter absorbs the whole of aerospace and space news.

**Spec/prompt drift note, 2026-08-22.** The `industrial_program` rule above existed in the classification prompt (`backend/agents/analyst.py`) since 2026-08-17, added in passing during other work, and had never been carried back here. The concrete consequence, observed at the measurement of 2026-08-22: two disagreements were read as gaps in the specification when the model was faithfully applying a written rule — the diagnosis therefore bore on the wrong object, and the fix proposed would have been to add a rule that already existed. **This document is the reference specification for the prompt**: a change to the classification rules that is not carried back here makes any precision measurement uninterpretable, since one no longer knows what the model is being evaluated against.

### Excluded (explicitly, to prevent perimeter drift)
- Classified intelligence, or closed / paid sources without a contract.
- Cybersecurity and information threats (a neighbouring but distinct perimeter, a subject in its own right).
- Automated decision-making or automatic corrective action (blocking, binding regulatory alert).
- Financial market analysis (share prices, company valuation) — outside the export/geopolitical risk subject.

### Grey areas that remain open
- A military movement outside the area but involving an export customer from the area (a delivery to a third country, say): in or out?
- Arabic- or Russian-language sources relevant to the Mediterranean area: covered in V1 or deferred to V2 (depends on the availability of an LLM reliable in translation/classification for those languages)?

## 5. Alternatives assessed (build vs buy)

| Option | Advantages | Limits for this use case | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A generalist SaaS watch tool (Meltwater, Onclusive, Netvibes Enterprise, say) | Fast deployment, no development | No multi-source cross-checking customisable to the export-control perimeter, no confidence score or bespoke guardrails, a recurring licence, strong vendor dependency on a sensitive business perimeter | Rejected |
| No-code automation (Make/Zapier/n8n + a generic LLM call) | Very fast prototyping, no infrastructure to manage | No explicit shared state between steps, fine-grained per-node traceability hard to obtain, guardrails (caps, step limits) hard to enforce robustly on a third-party platform | Rejected |
| Bespoke development on an agentic orchestrator (LangGraph) | Explicit shared state (`VigieState`) between the nodes, native per-node traceability (LangSmith), guardrails coded in, an architecture that absorbs adding the verifier (V2) and the queryable memory (V3) with no rewrite | Higher development and maintenance cost than a SaaS or no-code building block | **Chosen** |

The decisive criterion is not the speed of initial implementation but the ability to guarantee, verifiably, systematic traceability and cost caps — non-negotiable requirements (see section 8) that a generalist third-party building block cannot guarantee contractually.

## 6. Assumptions & constraints

- The RSS/specialised press sources targeted are publicly accessible and without scraping restrictions (to be checked legally per source — terms of use, robots.txt).
- The LLM (Claude) is judged reliable enough for classification and summarisation subject to an explicit confidence score and a human review of low-confidence cases — the system does not claim 100% reliability.
- LLM and infrastructure budget capped from V1 (a non-negotiable guardrail, see section 8).
- The end user systematically reads the daily digest before any action — the system is an accelerator, not a substitute for human judgement.

**First precision measurement (2026-08-11, n=30)**: 27/30 (90%) in raw agreement with the human annotation, above target. Re-examining the 3 disagreements against the literal definition of the categories rather than against intuition, only one was confirmed as a genuine gap in the classifier (opinion content wrongly classified into a thematic category — fixed, see the boundary clarifications in §4); the other two reflected category definitions still ambiguous at that time (the merger-and-acquisition / export-control / financial-analysis boundary), since clarified. The lesson to keep: at a sample of that size, a precision measurement is as much a test of the clarity of the definitions as of the quality of the classifier — the two must be audited together, not the second alone. `n=30` remains insufficient for strong statistical confidence; to be repeated at greater scale before considering this KPI validated.

**Second measurement (2026-08-14, n=88, after the sources were reconfigured by country)**: 73/88 (83%), slightly below target. The 15 disagreements surfaced two poorly defined category boundaries (`defense_diplomacy` vs `military_movement`, 4 cases; `defense_diplomacy` vs `out_of_scope`, 3 cases), corrected in §4 above. **Methodological caveat**: part of the sample (~20 items) was annotated with the help of a second opinion consulted during annotation rather than judged strictly independently — the 83% figure must be read as indicative, not as a measurement of independent agreement in the strict sense. A repeat with strictly independent annotation, after the prompt fix above, is needed before considering this KPI validated or not on this iteration of the sources.

**Third measurement (2026-08-16, n=68, a sample rebuilt after the boundary fixes)**: 51/68 (75%), **below target**. The overall figure hides two very different regimes, and that is the useful result of this measurement:

| Breakdown | Result |
|---|---|
| Perimeter decision alone (in / out) | 58/68 (85%) |
| Fine category, on the items judged in scope | 23/36 (64%) |

Noise filtering therefore meets the target, while qualifying the items that reach the digest fails one time in three. A single category carries most of the gap: `industrial_program`, recall 5/11, with leaks in every direction (classified `out_of_scope`, `arms_contract`, `defense_diplomacy` or `military_movement` depending on the case). What the six missed items have in common is that they are about a **capability under development or under through-life support** — a request for information ahead of a purchase, a force structure target, industrial cooperation, the refit of a vessel — which the model classifies from the visible actor (an army, two states, a navy) rather than from the subject of the article. It is the only boundary in the perimeter for which §4 states no rule: the errors have moved towards the unspecified definition, the two `defense_diplomacy` boundaries handled after the previous measurement no longer being dominant.

**Attribution of the six misses, clarified (2026-08-18)**: two of the six missed `industrial_program` items are the teaser truncation cases diagnosed in §11 — the frigate order announcement (classified `out_of_scope`) and the Australia–UK radar cooperation (classified `defense_diplomacy`), both from the same feed, both correctly classified on replay against the full text. They therefore stem not from the missing §4 definition but from the material available, which the reading above filed with the others. The fix divides: strengthening the boundary rule addresses at most four of the six misses, full-text fetching addresses two, already demonstrated by replay. It is the first time a KPI figure is attributed to that ingestion limit rather than to the prompt.

**Tooling (2026-08-18)**: `backend/eval/score.py` now computes precision, recall and F1 per category, the perimeter decision as separate precision/recall (88% / 83%, of which the 85% accuracy in the table above is the aggregate) and the full confusion matrix. This measurement's figures were computed by hand; they are now derived from the sample, and therefore reproducible identically on the next one. The matrix is kept rather than a mere count of disagreements because the *direction* of the error points to the fix: confusing two in-scope categories and letting out-of-scope material in are not corrected in the same place.

**Matching tooling (2026-08-20)**: this section's annotated-sample method is extended to *pairs* of items, for the two measurements that were waiting on it — the threading acceptance criterion (§10 V3 slice 1) and the calibration of an escalation threshold (§10, the verifier arbitration). `backend/eval/build_pairs.py` freezes a sample of 65 pairs: all 13 intra-thread pairs, since those are exactly the ones the criterion judges, and 52 candidate pairs drawn by IDF score band, without which a threshold would remain a judgement call. `annotate_pairs.py` collects a binary "same story" judgement — same parties, same operation, not same theme, that distinction being the whole object of the measurement — with an "unsure" option that leaves the denominator instead of being counted as a success. `score_pairs.py` reweights the rates by the real population of each band: the sample being stratified, a raw count would over-weight the high bands, deliberately over-drawn. The score measured is imported from `candidates.py` rather than rewritten, for the same reason as the import of `_tokenize` — two implementations of the same computation diverge at the first correction. No LLM call: the score is deterministic, the judgement is human.

**Methodological caveats**, in both directions. The annotation tool displays the system's answer before asking for the label (`backend/eval/annotate.py`): the agreement measured is therefore overstated, but the disagreements are all the more solid since they are explicit contradictions of the answer shown. Six initially annotated items were moreover re-done from scratch after a consistency check against the §4 definitions — four flipped, two were maintained; the measurement before that redo gave 69%. The sample stays small (n=68, of which 11 items in the category at issue) and rests on a single annotator: the direction of the diagnosis is clear, its magnitude is not.

The cross-checking rate is deliberately a tracking indicator and not a target to maximise: pushing it up artificially would encourage the system to over-weight subjects already widely covered at the expense of isolated weak signals, which would contradict the weak-signal detection objective.

**Two fixes of 2026-08-17, retested against the real LLM on 2026-08-17 (34 calls, a two-arm protocol: arm A = the prompt/`classify_item` of HEAD~1 read from git, arm B = the current code, on the same input to isolate the effect of the fix).**

The `industrial_program` boundary rule (recall 5/11 at the third measurement) was tested on the 6 missed items plus 5 controls already correct in the same category (11 items, 22 calls). A mixed result, not a clean fix: **3 of the 6 misses corrected** (a request for information ahead of a purchase, industrial cooperation, an Infodefensa item initially misclassified), **2 remain misclassified** unchanged, and **1 item flips from a correct answer (arm A) to `out_of_scope` (arm B)** — an unfavourable signal on that precise item, in a context where another control item (no. 30) separately shows that the same call can vary from one arm to the other for reasons unrelated to the text of the rule (a validation failure on an unrelated field, absent in arm B). The 5 controls all stay correct in both arms: no regression in the strict sense on the control population. The recall measured on this sample therefore goes from 0/6 (arm A, consistent with the third measurement) to 3/6 (arm B), without degrading the items already well classified — but with an isolated case of instability that prevents concluding to a clean fix.

**Regression diagnosed (2026-08-17)**: the item that flips comes from a feed (ESUT) whose native RSS teaser stops at 151 characters — well below the 500-character truncation applied to the other sources in the sample — and whose only retained sentence is a generic stock-market remark (revenue, profit, raised guidance); the article's real industrial content (an order for four frigates worth several billion euros, several submarine programmes, an investment in a production site) only appears further down the full text, out of the teaser's reach. Replaying the same call with the article's full text (fetched from the item's link, 1 call): the current prompt correctly classifies `industrial_program`, with a verbatim quote of the frigate order to support it. The boundary rule is therefore not at fault — applied to a complete excerpt, it works as intended. The flip is explained by the interaction between a teaser structurally too short to carry the article's industrial content (a risk factor already identified generically the same day, see §4 "snippets vs full articles") and a rule which, rightly, makes the judgement bear on the content actually visible rather than on the actor named — which mechanically exposes teasers with no explicit industrial content. **The independent precision measurement still to be redone (see below) will settle whether the net effect is positive at scale.**

The `diplomacia_defense` fix (silent loss of Infodefensa items through translation of the category identifier) was tested on 6 fresh Infodefensa items collected live (12 calls), not on the archived sample — the 3 items lost during the third measurement had left no usable trace (`build_sample.py` only writes items classified successfully). A clear, confirmed result: **3 of the 6 items reproduced the Spanish variant `diplomacia_defense`** in the model's raw response. In arm A (without the fix), 2 of those 3 items failed Pydantic validation and would have been lost silently — a direct reproduction of the third measurement's bug, on real and no longer retrospective content. In arm B, all 3 occurrences were normalised towards the correct identifier with no item lost. Fix confirmed, without reservation.

**A second source truncation case diagnosed (2026-08-17)**, on one of the two misses that stayed unchanged: an Australia–UK item (cooperation on a radar technology, a statement of intent signed by both ministries) stays classified `defense_diplomacy` on its native teaser — the initial hypothesis being a contradiction between the `defense_diplomacy`/`military_movement` boundary rule and the `industrial_program` one on declared inter-state cooperation. Checked and then refuted: the prompt already contains an explicit discriminator for that precise case (the building of a *named* programme, piece of equipment or joint force, including when the announcement takes the form of an official statement). The source's teaser (ESUT, the same feed as the regression above) stops before naming anything — neither the radar system, nor the manufacturers involved, nor the framework agreement; on that text, `defense_diplomacy` is the correct reading of the rule, not an error. Replayed with the article's full text: `industrial_program`, with a verbatim quote to support it, as the rule provides. The same diagnosis as the flipped item above — a structurally too short teaser, not a defect of the rule — on a different item from the same feed.

Consequence for the status: the `diplomacia_defense` fix is considered validated. The `industrial_program` fix remains **partially validated** — a positive net effect on the sample tested (0/6 → 3/6, no regression on the controls), and the two cases of instability observed (the flipped item and the Australia–UK item above) are now explained as source truncation artefacts rather than defects of the rule. Only one of the six original misses stays misclassified with no explanation. Status to be confirmed, not yet to be closed, pending the independent precision measurement.

**Fourth measurement (2026-08-22, n=48, the first blind annotation)**: 38/48 (**79%**), a 95% confidence interval (Wilson) of **[66%; 88%]**. The 85% target is **inside** that interval: this measurement therefore does not allow the conclusion that the product is below target — nor that it meets it. That is a difference of status from the third measurement, whose interval [64%; 84%] excluded the target and did establish a gap.

**This figure is not comparable to the previous one, and the 75% → 79% gap is not an evolution.** Three things changed at once: the annotation is blind (until that date, the annotation script displayed the system's classification before asking for the human judgement — an anchoring bias that pushed towards agreement, and therefore inflated the figure), the source composition is no longer the same, and the prompt has received the §4 boundary clarifications. None of the three effects is separable from the others on those two measurement points.

**The same boundary is pointed at by a second independent measurement — but the diagnosis had to be corrected mid-analysis.** Seven of the ten disagreements involve `industrial_program` — six out of nine counting the duplicate described below only once — and three bear on its boundary with `arms_contract`. §4 stated no rule on that boundary, which the measurement of 2026-08-16 had already noted; **but the classification prompt did carry one, since 2026-08-17, never carried back into this document** (see the drift note in §4). It placed "a delivery" on the `arms_contract` side — and on the clearest of the three cases, a manufacturer delivering its first example to an armed service, the model therefore **faithfully applied a written rule** the annotation contradicts. It was not a gap in the specification but a disagreement about it, and the two do not call for the same action: adding a rule that already existed would have changed nothing.

The underlying pattern, for its part, is confirmed and remains the one described on 2026-08-16: the model classifies from the **visible actor** — a customer navy, a receiving army — rather than from the **subject of the article**, a construction or delivery milestone. A vessel launched for the Peruvian navy and a surface drone delivered to the Bundeswehr came out as `arms_contract`; conversely, a government decision on down payments intended to secure delivery timelines came out as `industrial_program` when it is about the transaction and its funding — a case the prompt's rule did not cover at all.

**Decisions taken on 2026-08-22, and written into §4.** The rule changes: a delivery now belongs to `industrial_program`, and `arms_contract` tightens onto the commercial act itself — award, order, framework agreement, acquisition decision, funding. The split becomes a division into three stages — before the transaction, the transaction and its money, manufacture and the life of the capability — of which only the second is `arms_contract`. This is a **rule change and not the filling of a gap**: it follows the parent definition of `industrial_program` already present in the prompt ("the stage of building a capability"), of which a delivery is the culmination and not the transaction, signed elsewhere and earlier. A non-regression retest is owed before considering the rule settled — the lesson of the previous retests being that a rule's *position* in the prompt counts as much as its content, the formulation chosen leads with the `industrial_program` branch, the one that was losing the cases.

**A precedent that makes this fix predictable rather than hoped for.** The `defense_diplomacy` / `military_movement` boundary was the most frequent confusion in the sample of 2026-08-14; it was specified in §4 and then in the prompt, and it is today the best held in the sample — `military_movement` at 100% precision, F1 0.93, a single disagreement. Writing down a measured boundary does move the classifier's behaviour. The caveat that comes with that precedent also applies to the fix above: at that retest, the first formulation of the rule, which stated the same distinction, had moved **no** item out of 25 — only its rewriting leading with the branch that should win produced the effect. A rule's position in the prompt counts as much as its content, and a retest that checked only the reference case without its controls would not show it.

**Three disagreements belonged to the scoping and not to the classifier; they are settled, two against the annotation.** Customs duties on drone components had been annotated `export_control`: the category is now explicitly defined by the **legal instrument** — licence, sanction, embargo — and not by the economic effect, a tariff measure remaining `out_of_scope`, without which the perimeter absorbs the news of trade tensions. A warning from a **retired** general had been annotated `defense_diplomacy`: §4 now requires **official capacity at the time of the facts**, remarks by a former official committing no state and therefore falling under the opinion rule already written. Finally, two civil aerospace items had been annotated in opposite directions: an explicit rule attaches them to the perimeter **only** if the article states a defence link — an application, a customer, or industrial cooperation involving a defence group — the criterion being the link written down and not the presumed dual-use nature of the technology. The first two decisions give the system back the answer it had given.

**A figure that follows, not to be confused with the KPI.** The reference KPI remains **38/48 = 79%**: it measures agreement with an independent human judgement, and that is indeed what was measured. Against the specification as clarified that day, the system's conformity would be **40/48 (83%, 95% CI [70%; 91%])**, the two cases above proving it right. Both figures are true and do not say the same thing; neither settles against the 85% target, which stays inside both intervals. Never publish the second alone: rescoring a measurement against a spec written after the fact is legitimate for steering a fix, never for improving a result.

**A probe of the changed rule, the same day, on the two remaining calls.** The full retest was not affordable — it is owed — but the two reference cases pulling in *opposite* directions were replayed identically (the sample's excerpts are complete under the 500-character truncation, so the model's input is the same as at the measurement). Choosing two opposite cases is deliberate: two cases both pointing towards `industrial_program` would not distinguish a correct rule from one that swallows everything. **A split result.** The delivery of a first example to an armed service, which came out as `arms_contract`, now comes out as `industrial_program`: the rule change does produce the intended effect on the case that motivated it. But the government down-payment decision **has not moved** — still `industrial_program` where the new rule wants it as `arms_contract`, even though the rule names the case explicitly ("down payments made to secure timelines"). That is precisely the failure mode already observed at an earlier retest: a rule can be written, correct, and still have no effect. A hypothesis to check at the full retest, not to fix by judgement: the `industrial_program` branch was placed first to win the cases it was losing, and probably captures beyond its target as soon as an article names a capability and delivery timelines. **The rule is therefore not settled, and the current state is not a regression** — the funding case already came out that way before the change. No non-regression control could be run: n=2 says nothing about the rest of the sample.

**An ingestion defect found while checking the sample.** Two items in the sample are the same article, published by the same source under two identifiers — identical title and excerpt, different links. Deduplication being indexed on the link (`backend/memory/store.py`), it does not see them: in production, such a duplicate consumes two LLM calls, appears twice in the digest, and can form a thread of two items that are the same article. Counting the pair once, the measurement becomes 38/47 (81%), with no material effect on the conclusion. **Magnitude measured over the real history**: 0 exact-title duplicates over 295 items and 7 days. The defect is therefore real but not systemic at this volume — to be fixed when it costs something, not before.

**A sampling limit to keep in mind for any future measurement built on this draw.** The sample is stratified **by source** (`--per-source N`), not by category: `export_control` has only one reference item in it, missed, hence an F1 of 0 that concludes nothing. No per-category measurement is possible on the rare categories as long as the draw is built this way — yet `export_control` is one of the highest-value categories in the perimeter. The draw also keeps the N most recent items of each feed, which makes it a stratified convenience sample and not a probabilistic draw: the intervals quoted above hold as orders of magnitude, not as those of a random survey.

**Full retest of the `arms_contract` / `industrial_program` boundary (2026-08-23, 48 calls).** The retest owed since 2026-08-22 was run: 17 items from the frozen sample, two arms (the prompt of `d610568` against the reworded prompt), plus a third attribution arm and a stability control. Unlike the harness of point 36, both arms take the **same** invocation path — the rewording only touches the prompt, and varying the *repair* too would have measured two things at once. The 17 items: the 3 reference cases, 3 `arms_contract` controls, 7 `industrial_program` controls, 4 controls against spilling into the other categories.

**Two distinct defects found by re-reading the reference case against the text of the rule, before any call.** *(a) Position* — the `industrial_program` branch was stated first and expansively ("everything to do with… delivery, through-life support"), so that an article naming delivery timelines activated it before the funding clause, relegated to the end of an enumeration, was reached. *(b) Coverage* — the clause said "funds **voted**" when the reference case is about funds *requested* from parliament and not yet voted ("werde er dem Parlament einen Nachtragskredit beantragen"): it did not literally cover the case it named word for word. The second defect was not in the starting hypothesis, which bore only on position. Both are fixed together, §4 first and then the prompt.

**Result on the reference cases: the rewording hits its target, and a second one.** The Federal Council's down-payment decision flips from `industrial_program` to `arms_contract` — stable over 3 replays. The Peruvian launch, **never tested until now**, flips from `arms_contract` to `industrial_program` — stable over 3 replays — where the delivery clause of 2026-08-22 had not moved it: it really is the order of the split, and not the content of the clause, that wins that case. The surface drone delivered to the Bundeswehr stays `industrial_program`. **No regression introduced**: 15 of the 17 items are unchanged between the two arms, including the 7 `industrial_program` controls — the `arms_contract` branch, now leading, does not over-capture — and the 4 spill-over controls.

That is exactly what the retest discipline announced — "check the controls and not only the reference case" — and what a probe at n=2 could not see.

**A conclusion from 2026-08-22 does not reproduce.** It stated there that the delivery of a first example to an armed service, which came out as `arms_contract`, now came out as `industrial_program`, "the rule change does produce the intended effect". Replayed on the prompt from before the clause, on identical input (the excerpt is 410 characters, so it did not undergo the 500-character truncation), that case comes out as `industrial_program` **4 times out of 4**. The probe of 2026-08-22 was comparing a fresh call to the `category_system` recorded in the sample, that is one observation against one: the gap was replay variance, not the rule. **Measured balance of the delivery clause on this set: no gain, two regressions.** The right move was therefore not to keep it as it stood — but today's rewording leaves it in place, and deliberately so: settling it requires an arbitration that does not belong to the retest, see below.

**What stays open and calls for a human judgement, not one more call.** The South Korean patrol boat and the German surface drone are **the same type of event** — a manufacturer hands its first example to a customer armed service — and carry **opposite** annotations, `arms_contract` for the first, `industrial_program` for the second. No formulation of the rule can satisfy both: as long as that annotation inconsistency is not settled, "correcting" the delivery clause would amount to silently choosing which of the two annotations is right. Two possible outcomes, and they do not call for the same action: either the patrol boat's annotation is revised to `industrial_program`, and the delivery clause becomes defensible as it stands; or it is maintained, and the rule must acquire a distinction that neither §4 nor the prompt carries today — plausibly between handing over a series example under a contract being executed and the milestone of a programme still in trials, which the German article explicitly calls an `Innovationsvorhaben`. The Bulgarian case is distinct and simpler: the article is about the execution delay of an order already placed, which argues for `arms_contract` while saying nothing about deliveries in general.

**A methodological caveat, not to be sidestepped by quoting the wrong figure.** This set of 17 items is **deliberately enriched in disputed cases** of the boundary measured: agreement with the annotation goes from 11/17 on the `d610568` arm to 13/17 on the reworded arm, but those proportions are **not** comparable to the blind 79% over 48 items and must never be quoted as a precision. They say one thing and one only: on the items that carry this boundary, the rewording gains two cases and loses none. The precision KPI itself remains that of 2026-08-22 and **has aged** — it was measured on a prompt that has changed twice since, and remeasuring it would take a whole day's budget.

**First breakdown of `analyze` spending over a full batch (2026-08-30, 150 calls).** The prerequisite set on 2026-08-22 is lifted: the (source, outcome) breakdown had existed since that day but had only run on a batch of 3 items at the end of the budget, which said nothing about composition. That day's run covers **144 items submitted for 45 kept — 99 calls, that is 69% of the analysis cost, on discarded items**. Two interpretation caveats before the figures, both important. *(a)* It is not a normal day: the previous launch dated from 2026-08-22, that is a gap of 192 h, and the collection window only covers 96 — the batch is a catch-up, not a daily flow. *(b)* The per-source cap discarded 234 items across 7 feeds before analysis, and it keeps the most recent ones: high-volume generalist feeds (Yonhap, 117 recent items for 8 kept) are therefore sampled over the last hours of a four-day pool, not over the four days.

**What the breakdown shows, and which was not guessable from the total.** *The loss is diffuse*: the largest single-source loss is 10 calls out of 99 (Breaking Defense, NK News and Opex360 tied). No feed carries the discarded spending on its own, so no removal of a source corrects it. *The two reasons do not point to the same fix*, which is exactly what the (source, outcome) key was built to separate: `out_of_scope` weighs **72 calls** (50% of the day's budget) and belongs to source composition; `quote_unverified` weighs **26** (18%) and was charged to extraction, and therefore to `fetch_full_article` — **an attribution refuted on 2026-08-31, see below**. *One case stands out clearly*: **Opex360 loses 8 of its 10 items to `quote_unverified` and keeps none**. It is a French-language defence specialist, fully inside the perimeter — its problem is not the subject. The measurement of 2026-08-31 showed it is not the length of the excerpts either.

**The `quote_unverified` reason was misattributed (2026-08-31, 50 calls).** The breakdown above attributed those 26 calls per run to the brevity of the RSS excerpts, and named `fetch_full_article` as the fix. Checked in paired arms — 10 Opex360 and ESUT items run twice, once on their teaser alone, once on teaser plus full article: **of the 6 quote failures observed, 4 are pure typography** and 2 are genuine paraphrases. The model reproduces the source's content faithfully but normalises its marks — a straight apostrophe against a curly one, straight quotes against guillemets — and the verbatim comparison folded case and whitespace, not typography. The two paraphrases are beyond the reach of any extraction fix: their longest common fragment with the source is 7 and 12 characters, and the §8 guardrail is right to refuse them.

Folding those variants before comparison took the batch's retention from **2/10 to 5/10**, with not a single HTTP request nor one extra call. That is the correction the measurement called for; it is not the one it had announced. The lesson is the one already drawn in §4 on 2026-08-22: a measurement that names a fix without having isolated it sometimes points at the wrong object, and checking costs less than building the wrong one.

**What remains for `fetch_full_article`, on a different justification.** The module is shipped the same day, but for classification and not for the quote. Over the 10 paired items, the two favourable flips are two `out_of_scope` items that became kept items once the whole article was read — the class of defect noted at points 38/39, where a truncated teaser does not contain what is needed to classify. The full balance is **+2 gains and −1 regression**: pointing the expected way, but inconclusive at that sample size, hence a switch (`FETCH_FULL_ARTICLE`) rather than hard-wired behaviour. The regression is instructive and was recorded: concatenating the article to the teaser guarantees that a given quote stays verifiable, but not that the item's fate is stable — faced with a longer text, the model **picks a different quote**.

Three feasibility readings come with the module, two of which correct an earlier note. The first: **three sources refuse a bare GET** and answer 200 with a browser header (Breaking Defense, TASS, NK News), where the note announced two, one of which — Federal Register — in fact answers 200 with nothing special. Its problem is elsewhere: the extraction there brings back the site's legal notices and not the document. Only `Defense.gov` genuinely resists (403 either way) and is the subject of a named waiver. The second: the check that verifies an extraction really covers the article requested must anchor on the **teaser** and not on the title — a title is a summary, which the body of an article does not repeat, and title anchoring wrongly rejected 7 correct extractions out of 9. The third: the anchor score is **logged without deciding anything**, the only two failing cases being already caught by the "teaser too short to anchor" rule — no separation is left on which to calibrate a threshold, and setting one by judgement would be the arbitrariness dressed up as measurement that `THREAD_GATE_MIN_SCORE` avoided until it had its annotated sample.

**Six sources kept nothing at all — 38 calls for 0 items**: NK News (10), Opex360 (10), Jerusalem Post (8), Yonhap (8), Bruxelles2 (1), Federal Register – BIS (1). That observation does not convert into a removal, and the anti-mono-source drift guardrail says why: Jerusalem Post and Yonhap are generalists, whose presence in the panel serves the editorial diversity on which corroboration and the calibrated thresholds rest. A rule that followed yield would recentre the corpus on the most productive agencies — the exact effect the per-source cap was set to undo on 2026-08-17. The two sources at 1 call say nothing: the sample is too small.

**What this run does not settle, and what one must be careful not to make it say.** The arbitration of the budget split between the three nodes **stays open**: the history having been purged at the start of the run, the verifier's gate retained no item (0 escalations, 0 calls) and the threader's only 2 (6 calls). The three nodes were therefore not in competition that day, and the split cannot be measured on a run where two of them did not run. What the run adds to the file is elsewhere, and it moves the question: it is not only how to divide 200 calls between three nodes, but that **half the daily budget goes on `out_of_scope` and a fifth on unverifiable quotes** — two lines no redistribution between nodes recovers.

**Two side effects of the 8-day gap, recorded because the history alone will not show them.** Retention being 7 days and the purge running on every run, the 295 records accumulated from 2026-08-15 to 2026-08-22 were **all** outside the window on 2026-08-30: the run purged them entirely, and the history starts again at 45 items over a single day. No measurement is lost for all that — the corpora that depend on them (`sample.json`, `pairs.json`) have been frozen outside the store since 2026-08-20, precisely for this case. And deduplication discarded no item from the batch, not because the purge had emptied the link memory, but because the collection window (96 h) no longer overlapped the last collection (192 h): the two sets were disjoint.

## 8. Risks & guardrails

Priority = Probability × Impact; in decreasing order, the High/High risks handled first from V1.

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Priority | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLM hallucination (a statement with no real source) | Medium | High | **High** | Mandatory traceability: every statement in the summary must point to the source excerpt; automatic rejection of a summary with no quote |
| Misuse (the system perceived as validating an export/regulatory decision) | Medium | High | **High** | The deliverable is explicitly positioned as an aid to the watch, not as a decision or enforceable compliance tool — a legal and reputational risk for the using organisation in case of misuse |
| Source bias (over-representation of one area or one point of view) | High | Medium | Medium | Sources selected by country on an explicit criterion (§4), `state_affiliated` marking of state media, per-country coverage tracked, periodic review of the list. Any rule allocating collection by yield (making the per-source cap adaptive) stays **subordinate to this risk**: a strictly positive floor per feed, diversity treated as a constraint and not as an adjustment variable — the best-yielding source in the panel is a state agency, and a concentrated corpus thins out corroboration while distorting the thresholds calibrated on it (measured on 2026-08-16, see `docs/decisions.md`) |
| Cost drift (uncontrolled LLM calls) | Low* | Medium | Medium | A daily LLM budget/call cap (a guardrail from V1, also covering the verifier's calls) + a freshness window at collection, which prevents a feed with a deep history from submitting its whole backlog in one run. The cap **truncates** the run rather than cancelling it: the items already analysed are kept and served, those not submitted to the model stay collectable on the next cycle — a cost guardrail that destroyed work already paid for would turn against the objective it protects — *high probability if the guardrails are not active, hence their non-negotiable character |
| Runaway agent loop | Low* | Medium | Medium | A step limit per agent run (the graph's `recursion_limit`) **and** a double cap on each agentic node — verifier and thread grouping: items escalated per run, tool iterations per item. The first bounds the graph, the second bounds the loop internal to the node, which the `recursion_limit` does not cover; every new node with a tool loop must carry its own — *likewise, depends on the guardrails being active |
| Dependency on a single LLM vendor | Low | Medium | Low | A decoupled architecture (LangChain) allowing a model change without a complete rewrite |

## 9. Governance & adoption

- **Product decision-maker**: to be designated within the using organisation, probably the export compliance or competitive intelligence function — arbitrates changes of perimeter (sources, themes, the grey areas listed in section 4).
- **Place in the existing workflow**: a daily digest generated before the morning review starts, consulted ahead of any coordination meeting — the system feeds an existing human process, it does not replace it.
- **Division of roles**: the analyst remains the sole decision-maker for any action; the system prioritises and summarises, it neither validates nor invalidates a statement autonomously.
- **Training**: a reading guide for the confidence score and the classification must be circulated to users — a confidence score misunderstood (taken as a guarantee rather than an aid to prioritisation) would invalidate the non-goal guardrail described in section 3.
- **Feedback loop**: the false positives/negatives reported by analysts feed the improvement of classification, but any change to the prompt or the classification logic is validated manually — no silent automatic retraining, so as to stay auditable.

## 10. Delivery plan

- **V1** — collection + classification + text summary, fixed sources. Acceptance criterion: coverage ≥ 90%, a daily digest generated with no manual intervention, every element of the digest traced to its source.
  - *Production rollout — the repository half delivered on 2026-08-23, the cloud half on 2026-09-05.* The diagnosis of 2026-08-22 had found something other than the absence of infrastructure: the pipeline **logged nothing**, so all the budget instrumentation built over the previous two days would have vanished under a scheduler. Logging was therefore handled before infrastructure — a structured JSON log across the five nodes, the budget guardrail and the run endpoint, in a format designed to be filtered by field and not read by a human. Then came closing `POST /run` behind a shared token (it triggers the whole day's spending on its own), restricting the allowed origins, pinning dependencies exactly, a container image aligned with the continuous integration's Python version, and a batch entry point distinct from the service. **Architectural choice made**: the daily run is a *Job* with no request timeout — it takes ~620 s and that duration rises with the verifier's coverage — and the service carries only the digest already produced; the front is hosted separately. **What is verified**: the image built, the service exercised on its four expected responses, and the batch job run end to end against real feeds with the call cap forced to zero, which exercises truncation without spending anything. *Delivery of the cloud half, on 2026-09-05.* Managed database, service and batch job deployed, continuous deployment triggered by every code publication, interface hosted separately. The infrastructure is described in Terraform in a form that **adopts** what exists rather than recreating it: a database's region not being revisable after creation, a module written from scratch would have proposed destroying what was running. **The first real run** took 615 s for 156 articles submitted and 61 kept, consuming 195 of the 200 calls authorised, with no truncation. **Three checks only production can give** have passed: writing to the database, deduplication reading back from one run to the next what it had written, and the budget reservation **in a transaction under real concurrency** — 30 simultaneous reservations spread across 3 containers for 4 remaining slots, exactly 4 accepted and 26 refused. That last point was the only guardrail in the system that could be neither confirmed nor refuted anywhere but in production. **What still is not**: the daily scheduler was written but not armed until 2026-09-06 — every run was still triggered by hand — and the seven-day purge will only be observable on 2026-09-12, on the eighth day of history. The V1 acceptance criterion "a daily digest generated with no manual intervention" is therefore **not met until a full cycle has been observed**, for want of automation and not for want of capability.
- **V2** — verifier agent (cross-checking, confidence score) + interactive sectorised map. Acceptance criterion: a confidence score available on 100% of events, cross-checking rate measured and documented.
  - *First slice delivered*: the verifier node produces `model_confidence` and `corroborated` through a bounded tool loop (a search over the history of already analysed items, a 7-day window since 2026-08-20 — 30 days before that). Items not escalated come out with no score rather than with a default value — a score fabricated by heuristic would be more misleading than useful for human prioritisation (see §9, the risk of a misunderstood confidence score).
  - *Extension to the whole perimeter, and an escalation gate — wired on 2026-08-20.* Until that date, only `export_control` and `arms_contract` were escalated: a cost restriction, not a product one. It is lifted, and replaced by a measured condition — an item is only escalated if the history holds an antecedent whose IDF-weighted overlap reaches `VERIFIER_GATE_MIN_SCORE = 20` (`backend/config.py`, applied by `store.has_antecedent`). **What made the arbitration possible**: over the 261 items accumulated, with candidates restricted to earlier dates as `exclude_links` does, opening the five categories with no gate would cost ~36 escalations/day, that is ~71 daily calls against a cap of 200 shared with analysis — the impasse observed on 2026-08-16. The gate at ≥ 20 brought that, in projection, down to ~8 escalations/day (~16 calls) — **the first real run, on 2026-08-21, gave 15**, the projection having averaged a week whose first days started from an almost empty history; see §11. It **loses no corroboration over the week measured**: the only two corroborated items carry antecedent scores of 32.0 and 35.4, the two highest of the 20 scored items, while the 18 uncorroborated top out at 23.1. The inverse measurement to that of 2026-08-16, where the best match only arrived in 10th position behind six false positives — the corpus was not the same then (102 items dominated by a single source, before the source revision of 2026-08-17).
  - *An accepted consequence for the V2 acceptance criterion.* "A confidence score available on 100% of events" is not attainable under a bounded budget, and would only be so by paying a call to produce a non-answer where the history has nothing to say. The criterion is therefore **rewritten**: a score available on 100% of the items an explicit, measured eligibility rule retains — today the existence of a candidate antecedent. The rule is exposed on screen rather than hidden: an item with no score says which of the silences applies to it (no candidate antecedent, which is a measurement; the run cap or the budget, which is an absence of measurement; analysed before the extension). **Run for real on 2026-08-21**: 15 items out of 36 clear the gate and are all escalated, 5 carry an antecedent. The run did however hit `MAX_VERIFIER_ESCALATIONS_PER_RUN` (15) with no margin, and the daily cap of 200 calls for the first time in the campaign — see §11.
  - *Second slice delivered*: the interactive map, built on the `location` field extracted and verified verbatim. A choropleth of volume per country, digest filtering on click, per-category breakdown on hover. It also displays what it cannot place — items with no extractable place, places not attachable to a country (maritime spaces, straits, transnational regions) — and distinguishes four never-merged provenance levels (§11): country cited and verified, country inferred from a named place, country inferred from the protagonist for want of an attachable theatre, presumed domestic attachment. A map that showed only its successes would overstate the real coverage.
  - *Still to do on V2*: enriching the context by fetching the full text of the articles (the RSS excerpts are often truncated), and thematic sectorisation of the map — the per-category breakdown is today only readable on hovering a country, not rendered on the map itself.
- **V3** — longitudinal reasoning over the history. V1 and V2 handle each item in isolation: classification, summarisation and verification bear on one article at a time, and then the item is stacked into the history. Yet an important part of the information a watch is looking for lies *between* the items — a story that evolves over several days and several sources, the frequency of a country rising within a category, an industrial transaction that precedes an export-control announcement. This version makes the reasoning bear on the accumulated history rather than on the current item alone. Overall acceptance criterion: every statement produced at this level (thread, trend, alert) stays traceable to the items that found it, in the same way an item summary is traced to its quote (§8) — a trend not attachable to the items that produce it is rejected, not merely flagged.
  - *A prerequisite already in place*: the history of analysed items has become the product's single store — it feeds both the verifier's cross-checking and the digest served by the API, over a configurable sliding window rather than over the last run alone. V3 exploits that store, it no longer has to build it. Persistence is abstracted behind a single interface (local files in development, Firestore in production), a condition for that history to survive a deployment onto instances with ephemeral disks.
  - **Slice 1 — event threads. Delivered.** Group the items that cover the same story and render them as a chronology rather than a flat list. Builds on the cross-checking logic already constructed for the verifier (§10 V2). Acceptance criterion: a thread brings together only items about the same story (same parties, same operation), measured on a hand-annotated sample using the method already applied to classification precision (§7).
    - *What is built*: a `thread` node placed after `verify`, reusing the verifier's pattern — a tool loop bounded on two axes (items escalated per run, tool iterations per item), the same daily budget guardrail, with no separate counter. Two deliberate divergences: no per-category filter, `out_of_scope` never reaching the analysed history, and no exclusion of the current batch, two sources covering the same event on the same day being the clearest case of "same story" — where the verifier's corroboration on the contrary requires an independent confirmation over time.
    - *Escalation gate — set on 2026-08-20, after three successive measurements.* Initially, an item was only escalated to the model if at least one keyword-overlap candidate existed in the history: a numerical similarity threshold would have required a base of history not yet available, and setting it by judgement would have produced an arbitrary choice presented as a measurement. **Measurement of 2026-08-18**: that filter was cleared by 100% of items over a history of 199 items — the query being the whole title and summary, it almost always shares a token with at least one record in the window. It therefore did not constitute a second guardrail. **Measurement of 2026-08-20, over 261 items and 7 days**: the IDF-weighted score, by contrast, does discriminate — over 25,424 pairs of distinct dates, ≥ 40 retains only 8 items out of 261 (3.1%), ≥ 30 retains 32 (12.3%), ≥ 20 retains 88 (33.7%). What remained was where to cut, which eight pairs read by eye do not settle: a sample of 65 pairs stratified by score band was therefore frozen (`backend/eval/build_pairs.py`) and annotated by hand. **Annotation closed on 2026-08-20** (`backend/eval/score_pairs.py`): per band, the rate of true matches goes from 0% (0-10) to 12.5% (10-15), 37.5% (15-20), 50% (20-25), 75% (25-30), 87.5% (30-40). Reweighted by the real population of each band (the sample is stratified, and a raw count would over-weight the high bands, deliberately over-drawn), a threshold of ≥ 20 retains an estimated precision of 62.0% for ~62 candidate pairs a week — against 20.2% at ≥ 10, the old filter in practice. **A scale clarification, noted on 2026-08-20**: the calibration was produced by `backend/eval/candidates.py`, which weights in `log(n / (1 + df))`, while the threshold is applied by `store._overlap_score`, which weights in `log(n / df)`. The 52 annotated pairs rescored on the scale actually applied move 4 pairs by one band and give 62.0% at ≥ 20 (78.5% at ≥ 25, 93.6% at ≥ 30): the choice of threshold is unchanged, the figure published is not. **A caveat added on 2026-08-21**: that precision bears on annotated pairs and remains valid, but the *escalation volume* expected from this threshold was estimated on a history still ramping up — the same bias that distorted the verifier's projection (§11). Replay that rate before quoting it; the precision, no. `THREAD_GATE_MIN_SCORE = 20` (`backend/config.py`) therefore replaces the free filter, applied by `search_thread_candidates` through its `min_score` parameter. It only applies once IDF weighting is active (window ≥ 3 items) — below that, the score is a raw count with no scale relation, and the filter falls back to "at least one candidate" so as not to exclude the canonical thread case (two sources from the same run, history still empty).
    - *Display*: the timeline is drawn at the real scale of time and not in regular steps — the gap between publications is the signal, and equidistant nodes would make three dispatches in twenty minutes and a story spread over three weeks look identical. An article its feed does not date is positioned on its entry into the store, flagged as such, never presented as a publication time. No aggregate reliability indicator is produced at thread level: averaging scores of which some are `null` would fill by computation the gap the verifier deliberately leaves (§9). The provenance crossing keeps the outlet's country and the event's country apart, by the same rule as on the map (§11).
    - *Acceptance criterion: met on 2026-08-20.* The initial validation had been done against real data on one story followed by three sources, not on an annotated sample; the measurement was sequenced after the accumulation campaign. With that closed (7 days, 261 items, 11 threads), the tooling was written on the model of §7: `backend/eval/build_pairs.py` freezes the 13 intra-thread pairs — all of them, with no sampling, since those are exactly the ones the criterion judges — plus 52 candidate pairs stratified by score band; `annotate_pairs.py` collects the human judgement (same story: same parties, same operation, not merely the same theme); `score_pairs.py` derives the threading precision and the effect of a threshold from it. **Result: 13/13 intra-thread pairs judged the same story, precision 100%.** **What is measured is a precision, not a recall**: a story the model failed to bring together produces no pair to annotate, so the sample cannot count it. That result says "what the node groups is grouped correctly", not "threading brings together every story it should" — to keep in mind whenever this figure is quoted.
  - **Slice 2 — weekly brief.** A node that reads the last seven days of history, draws out the volume trends by category and by country against the previous week, and writes a summary of them. Acceptance criterion: every figure put forward comes from an aggregation function over the history and not from the model, and every statement cites the items that found it. This node consumes LLM budget: it must go through the same daily guardrail as the existing nodes (§8), and not have a counter of its own.
  - **Slice 3 — weak-signal detection.** Flag the unusual concentration of several corroborated items on one country/category pair within a short window. Acceptance criterion: an explicit, documented threshold, an alert attached to the items that trigger it, a false-alarm rate measured before exposing the function.
  - **Slice 4 — temporal display.** A horizontal time axis giving the volume series by category and by country over successive weeks — distinct from slice 1's per-story thread, whose granularity is the event and not the period. Display of the data produced by slice 2.
  - **Slice 5 — natural-language queries over the history.** Acceptance criterion: a historical query answered with citation of the original sources.
  - *Arbitration: which slices justify an agentic tool.* The verifier (V2) introduced the system's only tool, `search_related_items`, whose loop is bounded in code. Extending reasoning to the history does not justify making every slice agentic — three cases are distinguished: **a tool is justified when the model has to explore**, neither the number of calls nor the search terms being known in advance, the loop then being entrusted to it under a coded bound; **a single deterministic access does not justify a tool**, the data being injected directly into the prompt; **a pure computation justifies neither a tool nor a model call**, thresholds, counts and aggregations bearing on already structured fields. Applied to the slices: slice 1 falls under the first case (story matching is ambiguous and may require several queries); slice 2 under the second (the counters are supplied to the model, precisely so that it cannot invent a trend figure); slices 3 and 4 under the third. Making a step agentic when it does not need to be adds cost and a surface for error with no demonstrable benefit.
  - *Arbitration: no vector search to begin with.* The current cross-checking rests on keyword overlap, not on embeddings. Decision: keep that approach for the first slice, and only resort to a vector representation if keyword matching turns out to be insufficient in practice — a condition to be observed, not assumed. **First partial observation (2026-08-18)**: the defect measured was not the keyword approach but its weighting. Over 199 real items, 88% of pairs shared at least one token and 64% of the overlap score was carried by words present in more than a fifth of the corpus: the ranking of the five candidates served to the model was largely noise. Corrected by weighting each shared token by its rarity in the window (IDF), which derives from the corpus already loaded without introducing a threshold and without a stop-word list to maintain — a third of the candidates served change. That does not settle the embeddings question: the ground truth available is a single thread of three items, on which the ranking is unchanged. What is established is that a source of noise has been removed, not that the matching is sufficient. **Update of 2026-08-20**: the ground truth has grown to 11 threads (23 items), and the sample of pairs frozen that day is precisely what will settle it — if the annotation shows that true matches distribute badly along the IDF score (same-story pairs at a low score, noise at a high one), it is lexical matching itself that is at fault and not its weighting, and the condition set here for resorting to embeddings would be observed. The question therefore stays open, but it is now backed by a measurement rather than by intuition. At the volume observed (~110 items/day, that is on the order of 800 items over the 7-day window kept since 2026-08-20 — against 3,300 over the 30 days retained until then), a local index would suffice; resorting to a hosted vector database service is not envisaged at this scale. The search tool's contract would be unchanged, only its implementation would evolve.

## 11. Known limitations

**State at 2026-09-06.** **Today**: the whole codebase and the digest content moved to English — the category identifiers, the stored title field, both prompts. Three consequences to record rather than discover. *The precision KPI ages a third time*: it was measured on a prompt that has since changed twice, and the input text and the output language have now changed too; the 79% figure describes neither the current prompt nor the current output. *Records written before this pass carry the old field and category names* and are translated on read, a fallback removable once a purge has run on or after 2026-09-13 — the purge is the criterion, not the date. *And the digest is bilingual for a week*: the 61 records of 2026-09-05 carry French summaries, produced by the previous prompt, while everything collected from now on is in English. That is visible on screen and it self-heals with the retention window; it is stated here because a reader who sees it should know it is a transition and not a defect. **Also today**: the daily scheduler is declared and armed, and with it the two run alerts, which had been written as log queries on 2026-08-23 and had stayed queries. The V1 acceptance criterion "a daily digest generated with no manual intervention" is therefore within reach but **still not met**: it requires observing a full cycle with no intervention, which the first firing will give.

**State at 2026-09-05.** **That day**: the main limitation listed since the beginning is lifted — production persistence was executed against a real database, and with it the budget reservation in a transaction, verified under effective concurrency (30 simultaneous reservations across 3 containers for 4 slots, exactly 4 accepted). The system is deployed and serves its digest publicly. **Two new or displaced limitations**, not to be passed over in silence: the daily run is not yet automatic — the scheduler is written but not armed, so the V1 acceptance criterion "a digest generated with no manual intervention" remains unmet; and the seven-day purge is not observed, and will not be until 2026-09-12. **A lesson of method, rather than a figure**: the runbook prescribed for the concurrency check an instrument incapable of measuring — two full runs launched together, whose material deduplication had exhausted, produced **a single** reservation and did not even overlap. A counter left under the cap would then have passed for proof. It is the third recorded case here of a measurement that would have named its conclusion without having isolated its object.

**State at 2026-08-23.** Ten limitations are listed below. **That day**: the repository half of the production rollout is delivered and validated in a container (§10) — the pipeline now logs in structured JSON, which it did not do at all the day before. That closes a limitation never listed here and which would have weighed from the first automatic run: the budget measurements built on 21 and 22 August left the process only through the manual launch tool, which does not ship to production. The limitations below are otherwise unchanged, **including the main one**: production persistence has still never been executed. Three now carry a figure rather than an observation: the verifier (20 items out of 261 carry a score — a measurement taken before the extension to the five categories wired the same day), corroboration (2 antecedents among those 20), thread grouping (15 threads by the evening of 2026-08-21, sizes 4/3/2×13, acceptance criterion met — precision 100% over the 13 intra-thread pairs of the sample of 65 pairs frozen and annotated on 2026-08-20, which also made it possible to set `THREAD_GATE_MIN_SCORE = 20` in place of the free escalation filter). Note also that deduplication forbids re-analysis, so the items already in the history will never carry that field — the gain will only appear on the items collected afterwards.
